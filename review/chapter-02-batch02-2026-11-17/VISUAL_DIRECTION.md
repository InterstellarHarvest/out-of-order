# Batch2 — visual direction

The actual selected Batch1 files are the anchors, not an abstract retro-pixel specification. Use their cool slate and muted teal planes, fine contours, modeled enamel/aluminum, pale institutional plastic, layered botanical greens and restrained signal accents. First-pass oversized pixel blocks and flat graphic silhouettes were rejected. The owner accepted the revised tower rendering after seeing it beside the selected Bureau and Junction. Future batches should use the final v02 source prompts and selected reference pixels, not the rejected v01 studies.

Keep the enormous engineered greenhouse legible. Ribs, glazing, pale rails, ducts and the single mechanically rimmed sun connect the public exhibit, elevated offices, maintenance routes and transit system. The tower-left/exhibits-center/tram-right relation belongs to the Junction camera, not an invented compass plan.

The tower's first office is intact and orderly; a small decorative plant is not the later spore-inundated state. Garden flowers are cultivated specimens, with a stopped fan and a transparent duct as a useful common landmark. The damaged catwalk shares those beds, structure and duct while moving the camera above the spore level. Steel side stringers, intact handrail runs, missing grates and the lower lift landing must remain understandable.

C establishes the transit design selected from the Junction: cream molded shell/seats, teal inset pads/window pillars, steel floor and grab rails, slotted speaker plus tape mechanism, red brake, closed ceiling hatch and a right-side doorway. F uses the same camera and components; botanical passengers, seat roots and vine-sealed doors provide the authored change. The hatch is visible and remains usable. No humanoid conductor or other later reserve-tram character belongs here.

Moving Forest keeps biological bark, broad leaves and thorns distinct from the partially exposed steel guideway and root-bed carriage hardware. Numbered bark markers connect it to the service gallery. G introduces three large physiological gauges, an electromechanical trace, cable sockets and punched requests, with cables descending toward the central chamber. The complete flower and translator/district revelation remain for their own scenes.

For final exports, reuse the exact Batch1 64-paint palette rather than independently training a new palette. Fine pixel modeling is allowed; extra noise is not a style goal. Review strong silhouettes first, then repeated grating/foliage patterns at native scale. Use large readable pictograms or administrative bars for small plaques. Preserve raw output and record any local type polish with masks.

No selected asset changes. All new scenes are generated candidates awaiting selection; future art should reference the exact versions eventually chosen by the owner/Art Director.
