# M1 visual review 01 — aspect-ratio correction and style preference
**Date:** 2026-10-29. **Phase:** original visual mockup review, not UI lock or implementation authorization.

## Owner review of first three-state collage
- **Rejected top geometry:** the first/top image shows an extra-wide scenic viewport plus a near-square rail, making the *total effective game frame wider than 16:9*. Do not use this as a target layout. The collage dimensions are not a substitute for a game viewport.
- **Positive reaction to bottom two examples:** conversation with large detailed receptionist portrait, and document inspection state. Preserve their **artistic feel**, not assume their existing proportions are correct.
- Retain earlier selected **C+** contextual right rail, fixed scene framing, sprite-built mechanical casing, portrait, tactile choices, and paper documents.
- **Strong style signals:** cream and teal painted/enamel metal, brass fittings, screws, inset plates, chunky pixel-art detail, atmospheric sunny 1960s retro-futurist lobby. Decorative frames must look like *art assets that exist in the fictional world*, not generic CSS rounded-corner rectangles.
- The protagonist remains offscreen for testing; this is not a permanent protagonist presentation decision.

## Corrected next mockup constraints
1. Each mockup must be **one distinct, complete 16:9 game screen**. No misleading stacked collage or extra panel outside the widescreen bounds.
2. The **illustrated stage is approximately 4:3 within the 16:9 composition**, *not* 16:9 scenery with a sidebar added outside it.
3. To leave legible width for a substantial portrait, use roughly 70% scene / 30% rail as an **initial visual test**. This means the scene may be inset slightly vertically; preserve its 4:3 geometry. Alternative 75/25 only if portrait/dialogue stays readable.
4. Match the same fictional reception scene, lighting, scale and NPC between **conversation** and **document** images. Both states share identical panel size and stage alignment; only the rail changes content.
5. Dialogue state: large detailed inset portrait, brief speaker text, three tactile primary narrative choices, smaller secondary action(s). No permanent card deck or point-and-click verb grid.
6. Document state: portrait is replaced with an enlarged bureaucratic appointment notice/slip, close/back/navigation in the same rail. Stage must not move.
7. Later test the **quiet narration** state for visual emptiness: retain crafted physical panel structure and concise narrative, avoid filler meters/ornaments.
8. Text in generated visual art should be treated as **conceptual placeholder**: final UI copy and readability must be evaluated separately, not judged by approximate lettering.
9. Mockups are for owner's visual review, not finished game assets or UI implementation. Don't silently lock iconography, panel proportions, city name, or institution name.

## Next decision points after corrected visuals
- Does the corrected 16:9 stage/rail ratio still leave enough room for a large portrait?
- How much panel metal/trim is charming before it becomes visually busy?
- Should documents occupy almost the whole rail or a smaller framed reading surface?
- Is the quiet state intentionally composed when the portrait/choices are absent?
- Does the art feel like a 1960s imagined future, while preserving readable bright chunky pixels?

**Current verdict:** Favor the **style of lower conversation/document views**. **Top geometry fails**. New one-screen-at-a-time 16:9 revisions are needed. This records visual feedback, not a final art lock.
