# Interaction and storytelling possibilities
Status: options, not rules or a committed feature set.

## What to carry from a physical CYOA board game
*Choose Your Own Adventure: House of Danger* inspired a book-like branching story made more interactive by conditional checks, chance, and objects retained across scenes. That is useful **design inspiration**, not a request to duplicate its mechanics or card interface.

## A possible interaction loop
Observe a staged scene → interact with a person or object → choose an approach → sometimes make a risky check or use a tool → acquire knowledge, access, relationships, or complications → branch, return, or converge elsewhere.

The actual balance among reading, scene inspection, movement, item use, and chance has not been chosen. Avoid designing a full parser, party RPG, point-and-click inventory puzzle engine, or tabletop system on assumption.

## Choices
Choices should lead to consequences, not always correct/incorrect answers. An early decision may influence whom a character trusts, which area opens, what another witness believes, or whether a more dangerous approach becomes necessary. Discoverable alternatives should reward preparation, curiosity, attention, and creative item use.

## Risk resolution: possible, uncommitted
Dice could decide whether a dangerous climb, hurried repair, bluff, pursuit, escape, or improvised experiment succeeds. Preparation and acquired objects can improve odds. Scientific claims themselves are not chosen randomly by dice. Failure often ought to introduce a complication, cost, or different route rather than cause an immediate dead end. Exact dice system, stats, visible probabilities, and number of checks remain unresolved.

## Persistent resources
Provisional categories: access credentials, tools, physical evidence, reports, favors, relationships, injuries/conditions, and institutional suspicion. These could be tracked under the hood and displayed as a simple inventory and journal rather than deck cards. Any resource must justify its UI complexity.

## Branching without explosion
A braided structure is one candidate: meaningful routes that sometimes reconverge into shared milestones with altered world state and recolored interpretations. Some cases are self-contained and unrelated; not every oddity is a piece of one puzzle. A run might conclude without complete understanding. Replay should offer meaningfully different findings, not merely more collected badges.

## Failures, comedy and horror
The wrong person may bluff, misunderstand professional vocabulary, receive accidental authority, or exploit bureaucratic contradictions. Avoid making them a fool whom the game constantly humiliates. NPCs may be competent and sincere. Horror should arise from revealed consequences of reliable but misunderstood technology, not arbitrary 'anything goes' reality changes.

## Open decisions
- Adventure/gamebook scenes vs click-to-investigate vs character walkaround?
- How often do portraits appear? Can several speakers be shown?
- Does the player have a speaking portrait or avatar? Currently unresolved.
- Inventory object grid, journal, dossier, or card-like display?
- Is there any random resolution? If so, what stakes and player agency?
- Length, chapter structure, pacing, saves, and replay model?
- Is the underlying mystery fully solvable, partially solvable, or dependent on choices?
- How is risk signaled fairly without eliminating surprise?

No code, content format, state schema, or production system is authorized by this note.
