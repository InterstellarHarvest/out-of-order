# Idea archive — preserve paths not taken
Status: historical brainstorming record, not a backlog. Updated 2026-10-29.

Purpose: Keep candidates, alternatives, and ideas moved out of the current center of gravity. 'Parked' means not currently favored, **not forbidden**. Only owner statements determine actual rejection.

## Genre exploration in order
1. Retro CYOA with low logical resolution and integer scaling, stronger 16–32-bit art versus older SSS/HHH.
2. *House of Danger*-inspired branching decisions, event/challenge uncertainty, inventory and long-lived advantages.
3. Candidate tones: supernatural house, sci-fi station mystery, time-slip history, roadside/small-town weirdness, wilderness/survival, pulp adventure.
4. Film influences considered: *Brazil* (absurd retro bureaucracy), *Jurassic Park* (wonder, creatures, scientific arrogance, loss of control), space horror (isolation, dread), later surrealism.
5. Owner selected: absurd moving toward disturbing, with strange unpredictable revelations; science can give reliable observations without complete causal certainty.
6. Owner selected: a strange world with **one underlying mystery**, not a purely disconnected anthology or only a single claustrophobic central case.
7. Owner selected: **the completely wrong person** as protagonist.
8. Owner selected: an alternate retro-futuristic world where oddities are normal.
9. Owner selected: civilization built on a misunderstanding of a genuinely effective discovery.
10. Owner favored: **The Exchange**, but not locked plot; maintain 1960s future aesthetic and an ordinary person displaced by coincidence/bureaucratic nonsense.
11. Latest UI input: King's Quest VI-type scene layout and inset detailed NPC portrait; protagonist visible sprite unresolved. Avoid default deckbuilder/card-battler UI.

## Earlier pitch titles and settings (parked)
- **Unscheduled Fauna:** off-world biological preserve, too many creatures on the inventory, no one postpones opening; corporate hazard and creature encounters.
- **Department of Unusual Affairs / Office of Exceptions:** bureaucratic investigator assigned apparently impossible cases.
- **The Last Inspection:** research station with absent crew, anomalous machinery and corporate denial.
- **The Listening Post / Blackwater Atlas / The Red Archive:** other project brainstorming around scientific anomalies in astronomy, deep ocean and Mars, respectively; not current CYOA premise.
- **The Exposition of Everything:** glamorous scientific attraction increasingly unable to explain its own exhibits.
- **The Institute of Last Resort:** underground laboratories with incompatible accounts of experiments.
- **Meridian:** city grown out of a world's fair, containing daily life beyond the research institution.

## Illustrative case concepts (not chapters yet)
- **The Demonstration:** an automated exhibit 'refuses' to perform and files a complaint.
- **The Conservation Problem:** a specimen has a long documented history but a recently built enclosure.
- **Department of Previous Events:** records seem to describe future incidents.
- **The Last Department:** contradictory organizational charts conceal an authority everyone obeys.
Each could be adapted, merged, or discarded once the Exchange rules are coherent.

## Other overarching answers (parked, not disproven)
- **The Margin:** precision technology transfers error or unwanted variation elsewhere.
- **The Forecast:** predictive machinery makes predicted outcomes increasingly likely, threatening choice.
- **The Observer Effect:** routine experimental procedures affect the phenomenon they supposedly only measure.
- **Scale-up problem:** a reliable real discovery was widely applied outside its tested operating conditions.
The Exchange is currently preferred for adventure variety and unsettling questions about equivalents, memory, and identity.

## Other protagonist approaches (not selected)
Reluctant insider, unlucky visitor, professional investigator. The chosen 'wrong person' can borrow elements from these without making the protagonist a secretly qualified official.

## Earlier mechanics and presentation ideas (not chosen)
- Two-dice-plus-stat tests, character stats such as Nerve/Wits/Charm/Luck.
- Physical clue/item/condition/event *cards* as collectible images, a deck or hand.
- A walkable in-scene player sprite with traditional third-person adventure controls.
- Exact 320x180 or 256x144 runtime resolution.
- A 45–90-minute first run, 3–6 acts, ~30–60 choice nodes, fixed ending count.
These came from brainstorming, not requirements. Particularly, the user explicitly questioned card-heavy digital presentation and a visible protagonist sprite.

## Things explicitly not pursued at this stage
- Game implementation, level production, final character names, locked plot twists, or engine selection.
- A generic sci-fi mystery or educational quiz-case clone.
- Borrowing actual copyrighted settings, characters, and assets from reference games or films.
