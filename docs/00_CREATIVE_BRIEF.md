# Creative brief — living direction, not a design bible
Status: exploratory, owner-guided. Updated 2026-10-29.

## One-sentence idea
A retro-futurist pulp choose-your-own-adventure in which an ordinary nobody accidentally gains an official role in a society built on astonishing technology whose actual operation may be fundamentally misunderstood.

## Selected creative preferences
- **Protagonist:** the completely wrong person. Average and unexceptional, accidentally thrust into events through coincidence, identification confusion, or bureaucratic nonsense. Not secretly a predestined genius, hero, or chosen one.
- **World:** alternate 1960s-imagined future. Strange technologies and oddities are ordinary. Grand optimism, aging machinery, ridiculous institutions, colorful public spectacle, and a lived-in society coexist.
- **Tone:** starts absurd and funny, gradually becomes disquieting or horrifying, with unpredictable discoveries and occasional wonder. Not constant grimness.
- **Mystery structure:** a strange world of partly independent incidents, with one deeper thread. Some oddities should be mundane, benign, mistaken, unrelated, or genuinely beautiful.
- **Central theme:** civilization has discovered something remarkable and built upon an incomplete or incorrect understanding of what it does. Results may be repeatable and useful while the causal explanation remains mistaken.
- **Leading explanation:** The Exchange. The supposed transport technology may not move originals as believed, but exchange them for seemingly equivalent counterparts. This is a *preferred starting hypothesis*, not canon.
- **Presentation:** 16- to early-32-bit-inspired pixel art: richer than the older, roughly 8-bit approach of Space Sprout Sleuth / Hunger, Harvest, & History. Draw at a low logical resolution; enlarge with integer scaling and crisp pixels.
- **UI:** King's Quest VI is an especially relevant scene-composition reference. NPCs can speak through a separately inset/framed, higher-detail chunky-pixel portrait while environments remain lower resolution.
- **Gameplay intention:** interactive branching adventure, potentially with uncertainty, collected useful objects, and persistent consequences; NOT a deck-builder, card battler, or game whose interface revolves around a hand of cards.

## Creative promises
1. Explore a place worth visiting independently of the mystery: bureaucracy, working neighborhoods, scientific attractions, transit systems, odd wildlife, public life, and dangerous forgotten infrastructure.
2. Players are not guaranteed the full explanation. Observations, hypotheses, and evidence remain distinct. A mystery should be internally coherent even when characters misunderstand it.
3. Scientists and engineers can be careful, principled, and correct. The joke is overconfidence, incentives, group assumptions, or institutional procedure, not science itself.
4. The ridiculous can later acquire disturbing significance without implying every silly detail was secretly a clue.
5. Gameplay choices should matter. Dice, if used, should affect risky *actions*, not randomly determine scientific truth.
6. No borrowed copyrighted characters, exact interfaces, settings, or art from the cited films and games.

## Anti-goals and cautions
- Not another educational detective game built around assembling fixed evidence and choosing one predetermined science answer.
- Not a generic corridors-and-dialogue mystery; keep pulp travel, environments, escapes, creatures, and spectacle possible.
- Not a game that explains everything with a simulation, dream, omnipotent malicious AI, or single conspiracy by default.
- Not a Slay the Spire / Balatro-style card mechanic simply because House of Danger uses physical cards.
- Not automatically a point-and-click game or a protagonist-sprite walkaround; presentation and interaction are open.

## Uncommitted
Exact plot, final truth, world geography/name, character names, whether travel is spacebound, scene layout, protagonist visualization, resolution, dice, inventory UI, engine, scope, and eventual title.
