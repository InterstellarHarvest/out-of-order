3. **Separate the objective visually from inventory.** Preserve the screenshot’s idea of the objective as its own small framed panel, with a thin but visible **physical gap/divider/break in the housing** between it and the inventory rather than one continuous fused strip. Objective text is real UI content (contextual and dryly satirical), not prohibited frame text. Keep a separate, exclusively decorative mechanical right end-cap beyond the objective.

The latest overly thin version erased the mildly absurd *Brazil* / 1960s retrofuture character. Next pass must be **between those two**, selectively thicker at purpose-built joints, eccentric hinge, mismatched repair panel, offset bolts, questionable access hatch, quirky mechanical scroller; **thin around most content apertures**.
5. **Don't solve the character problem through writing.** Slightly off/illogical engineering must be expressed through sprite shapes, fasteners, rerouted cables, ill-fitting cover plates, odd proportions, mounting choices and wear—not by adding funny slogans *on the common frame*.
6. **Proportions remain fixed.** True 16:9 display; ~70% upper scene/~30% upper right rail; framed high-detail portrait with side rail scroller beginning **below portrait and ending above the three utility buttons**, not next to portrait. Vertical scrollbar space is continuously reserved; idle thumb centered, active thumb proportional. No ornamental top title banner.
7. **Sprites, not bland boxes.** Frame is a reusable authored pixel-object and remains crisp, slightly more detailed than the main scene but cohesive with portrait density. Not polished futuristic glass, generic steampunk, heavy grime or useless decorative clutter. Entire UI remains legible, English-only.
